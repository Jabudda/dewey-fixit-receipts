  1. True or False? #6TRUELOCKED

    Play-Doh started out as wallpaper cleaner.

    Answer and sources unlock when the Short goes live. Guess first!

    From the 1930s, Kutol Products in Cincinnati sold a doughy putty for wiping coal soot off wallpaper. When homes switched to cleaner heat and washable wallpaper, sales collapsed. Nursery-school teacher Kay Zufall used it for crafts with her students and helped name it Play-Doh, which went on sale as a toy in 1956.

    1. Smithsonian Magazine: The Accidental Invention of Play-Doh (2019)
    2. HuffPost: Play-Doh Was Originally Meant For Something Very Different
  2. True or False? #4FALSELOCKED

    In a duel, the gunslinger who draws second always wins.

    Answer and sources unlock Tuesday, Nov 10 at 10:00 a.m. Central, when the Short goes live. Guess first!

    Physicist Niels Bohr suggested it, and the story goes he won toy cap-gun duels to prove it. In 2010, University of Birmingham scientists tested it: reactive movements were about 21 milliseconds (10%) faster. But noticing the other person move takes roughly 200 milliseconds, so the one who moves first still wins.

    1. Welchman et al., The quick and the dead: when reaction beats intention, Proc. R. Soc. B (2010)
    2. University of Birmingham news: The quick and the dead (2010)
    3. EurekAlert! press release (2010)
  3. True or False? #3FALSELOCKED

    A theater flashed "Eat Popcorn" too fast to see, and popcorn sales jumped 57%.

    Answer and sources unlock Monday, Nov 9 at 6:00 p.m. Central, when the Short goes live. Guess first!

    Market researcher James Vicary announced the result in 1957. Nobody could replicate it, the theater's manager later said no test was ever done, and in 1962 Vicary admitted it had been a gimmick. It still started decades of panic about subliminal ads.

    1. 4A's: Of Sex and Ice Cubes, the Great Subliminal Advertising Scare
    2. Reason: Flashback (2000)
    3. Wikipedia: James Vicary
  4. True or False? #2TRUELOCKED

    The CIA tried to make Fidel Castro's beard fall out.

    Answer and sources unlock Monday, Nov 9 at 3:00 p.m. Central, when the Short goes live. Guess first!

    The US Senate's Church Committee (1975) described a CIA plan to dust Castro's shoes with thallium salts, a hair-removal poison, during a trip abroad, to ruin his bearded image. He canceled the trip. Other ideas included poisoned cigars and a contaminated diving suit. Castro kept his beard until he died in 2016, at 90.

    1. US Senate Church Committee, Alleged Assassination Plots Involving Foreign Leaders (1975), findings (PDF)
    2. NBC News: the CIA's Castro plots
    3. Inquirer/AFP: Killing Castro, poisoned cigars and explosive seashells
  5. True or False? #1TRUELOCKED

    A famous psychologist built a missile steered by pigeons.

    Answer and sources unlock Monday, Nov 9 at 10:00 a.m. Central, when the Short goes live. Guess first!

    In World War Two, B. F. Skinner's Project Pigeon trained three pigeons to peck at screens inside a glide bomb's nose cone to keep it on target. It was cancelled in 1944, revived by the Navy as Project ORCON in 1948, and dropped in 1953. Skinner (died 1990) received the 2024 Ig Nobel Peace Prize for it.

    1. Smithsonian Magazine: B.F. Skinner's Pigeon-Guided Rocket
    2. B. F. Skinner, "Pigeons in a Pelican," American Psychologist, 1960 (PDF)
    3. Improbable Research: Ig Nobel Prize winners (2024 Peace Prize)

  10. Ask Andy?

    Will your AI snitch on you?

    Police generally need a warrant to search a seized phone (Riley v. California, 2014). In United States v. Heppner (S.D.N.Y. 2026), a defendant's own chats with a consumer AI were found not to be attorney-client privileged. Educational satire, not legal advice.

    1. Cornell LII: Riley v. California, 573 U.S. 373 (2014)
    2. HSF Kramer: US v. Heppner summary
    3. Proskauer: SDNY on privilege and public AI tools

    Why don't we just nuke a hurricane?
